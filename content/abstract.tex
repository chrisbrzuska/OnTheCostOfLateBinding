In key exchange protocols, the derived key should be bound to the public data which uniquely identifies a session and its key material. In TLS 1.3, this binding happens rather late, which has been identified as a significant source of complexity for analysing the TLS 1.3 key schedule (ASIACRYPT 2022).

We argue that the extension of TLS 1.3 would be a natural point to adopt early binding. We show that the computational costs of an early binding to both the ciphertext and public key of the KEM as well as the key shares of the DH is negligible and demonstrate the significant difference in complexity of the analysis by providing formally verified proofs of two versions of a toy key exchange protocol, one with early and one with late binding. We model the key exchange protocol in the state separating proofs framework (SSP, ASIACRYPT 2018), and use the recently published Domino tool to verify our proofs.
